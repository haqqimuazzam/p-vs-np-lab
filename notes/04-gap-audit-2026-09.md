# Gap Audit — September 2026

## Status

This note records a research checkpoint after auditing the witness-finding route against the literature and checking recent 2025–2026 complexity-theory work.

## 1. No unconditional P vs NP proof found

The current literature still treats P vs NP as open. Recent work continues to develop conditional routes, barriers, proof-complexity lower bounds, and meta-mathematical approaches rather than a completed separation.

## 2. Witness-finding route: exact obstruction

Kawachi–Rossman–Watanabe prove an \(\Omega(n^2)\) lower bound for nonadaptive randomized witness finding in a black-box model, including NP queries, and an \(\Omega(n^2)\) lower bound for intersection queries when the hidden witness set is affine.

This is strong, but it does not imply P != NP. A black-box algorithm sees only oracle answers about a hidden witness set; an explicit SAT algorithm receives the entire formula and may exploit its structure. Therefore a black-box query lower bound cannot simply be promoted to an unrestricted time lower bound.

## 3. Why the affine-SAT idea currently fails as a P vs NP bridge

For an explicit affine system Ax=b over F_2, Gaussian elimination finds a witness in polynomial time. Thus affine instances are useful for studying query models, but they cannot themselves witness P != NP.

To obtain a genuine P vs NP consequence, a restricted computational model must be justified as a faithful restriction of arbitrary polynomial-time SAT algorithms. The present black-box model does not provide that bridge.

## 4. Candidate-compressor route is closed as an independent shortcut

A polynomial-time algorithm that outputs polynomially many candidate assignments and guarantees one satisfying candidate on every satisfiable formula exists iff P=NP, by SAT verification in one direction and SAT self-reduction in the other.

Therefore a universal theorem saying every such compressor can be diagonalized against is essentially another formulation of P != NP, not an easier lemma.

## 5. Fixed-point construction audit

The tempting construction phi = B AND D_{f(phi)} does not force a literal syntactic fixed point for an arbitrary polynomial-time f. Kleene-style recursion gives behavioral/program equivalence, not equality of the encoded SAT instance. A concrete polynomial-time f can avoid every literal fixed point of this transformation.

## 6. Barrier check

Any proposed unrestricted proof must address the classical relativization, natural-proofs, and algebrization barriers. Modern work continues to identify additional limitations around circuit lower bounds. Consequently, a proof based only on ordinary diagonalization, generic oracle reasoning, or standard algebraization is not expected to settle P vs NP.

## 7. New lead: feasible Kolmogorov complexity

A March 2026 revision by Kabanets and Kolokolova studies Kolmogorov's approach through chain rules for time-bounded conditional Kolmogorov complexity. This is a legitimate research direction, but the chain-rule statement is a hypothesis/target rather than an established proof of P != NP.

## 8. New lead: proof complexity / Extended Frege

Recent work by Pich and Santhanam gives conditional implications from suitable Extended-Frege lower bounds and related bounded-arithmetic hypotheses to P != NP. These results sharpen the target but do not supply the missing unconditional lower bound.

## Current conclusion

No valid gap-to-proof has been found in the routes audited so far.

The most defensible next target is not to claim a solution, but to attack a sharply formulated missing bridge:

> Find an explicit computational model M, weaker than unrestricted polynomial time but strong enough that a superpolynomial SAT lower bound for M would imply P != NP, and prove the lower bound unconditionally.

A successful result here would be a genuine intermediate theorem. At present, no such theorem has been established in this project.
